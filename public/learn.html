1

Segment

One line = one segment. Starts with a 3-letter code (MSH, PID, PV1â€¦). Each carries a specific kind of information.

2

Field

Parts of a segment, separated by the pipe |. Each position has a fixed meaning (PID-5 is always the patient name).

3

Component

Parts of a field, separated by ^. Patient name DOE^JOHN^A = Family DOE, Given JOHN, Middle A.

4

Repetition

Repeating values inside a field, separated by ~. Multiple phone numbers: 555-1111~555-2222.

The five delimiters

HL7 uses five special characters to break up a message. They're declared in MSH-1 and MSH-2 â€” so they can technically change per message!

|
Field Separator
Separates fields. Declared in MSH-1.
PID|1|123|DOE
^
Component Separator
Separates parts of a field. Declared in MSH-2.1.
DOE^JOHN^A
~
Repetition Separator
Separates repeated values. Declared in MSH-2.2.
555-1111~555-2222
\
Escape Character
Marks escaped sequences. Declared in MSH-2.3.
\F\ = literal pipe
&
Subcomponent Separator
Separates parts of a component. Declared in MSH-2.4.
AUTH&MRN

Common pitfalls

Mistakes that trip up newcomers and experienced engineers alike.

âš 

Assuming standard delimiters

Most systems use |^~\&, but the spec allows any. Always read MSH-1 and MSH-2 before parsing.

âš 

Confusing empty fields with missing ones

PID|1||X means field 2 is empty. PID|1|X means field 2 is X and field 3 doesn't exist. Both are legal.

âš 

Trimming whitespace that matters

Never trim spaces inside field values â€” some systems use them as part of the value. Only trim around the delimiters.

âš 

Ignoring the version field

MSH-12 tells you the version. Field meanings change between 2.3 and 2.5.1. Always parse version-aware.

âš 

Assuming CRLF everywhere

HL7 uses \r as segment terminator. Some systems send \r\n or just \n. Normalize first.

âš 

Sending PHI over unencrypted channels

HL7 messages often carry names, MRNs, and dates of birth. Never log them in plaintext or send over non-TLS connections.
